01Do we have to move off our RL environment?

No. We sit above HUD, Invisible, Scale, OpenEnv or your own harness and work on the verifier inside it. Nothing about your environment, compute or weights changes.

02Can we bring a verifier we already wrote?

Yes — that's the most common starting point. It goes through the same attack sweep and certification as one we compile ourselves, and you get the exploit traces either way.

03What does the red team actually see?

Only the verifier's interface: task, trajectory in, reward out. Never the source, the author, or how it was compiled. That separation is what makes the certificate meaningful.

04How does production data reach you?

Through the CC connector beside your gateway. Redaction and field mapping are agreed before anything moves, and the mapping is inspectable at any time.

05What if a task can't be verified deterministically?

We mark it uncertifiable and say so on the certificate. Approximating human judgment with a fragile proxy is exactly how reward hacking starts.

06When does a certificate expire?

On policy change, on a verifier revision, or when monitored drift crosses the threshold in the report. Expiry is loud: training pauses until it's re-certified.
